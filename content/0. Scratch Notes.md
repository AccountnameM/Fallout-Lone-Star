## AI Fallout: Texas ideas

- **The Dallas–Fort Worth Metroplex** — A sprawling ruin where skyscrapers have collapsed into one enormous urban wasteland. Former highways are dangerous trade routes controlled by competing factions.
    
- **Houston’s flooded ruins** — Parts of the city are submerged after catastrophic storms and infrastructure failures. Survivors live on elevated structures, oil platforms, and improvised floating settlements.
    
- **The Texas Oil Barons** — A faction descended from pre-war energy corporations that controls refineries and hoards fuel. They see oil as both currency and political power.
    
- **The Republic of Texas Rangers** — A militarized group descended from Rangers and emergency services. They protect settlements but have increasingly authoritarian ideas about maintaining order.
    
- **The Alamo** — Rebuilt into a fortified settlement and historical shrine. Different groups dispute who has the right to control it.
    
- **The Panhandle** — Vast plains filled with abandoned farms, wind turbines, dust storms, mutated cattle, and isolated settlements.
    
- **The Gulf Coast** — Tropical, swampy, and heavily mutated. Sea creatures have moved inland, while enormous storms periodically reshape the coastline.
    
- **Big Bend** — A spectacular but extremely dangerous desert region. Hidden military facilities and pre-war research installations could be scattered through the mountains.
    
- **Austin** — A relatively prosperous settlement built around surviving universities, music venues, and technology communities. It could have a strong independent culture distinct from the rest of Texas.
    
- **San Antonio** — A major trading hub built around the ruins of the River Walk, with parts of the underground tunnels turned into settlements.
    
- **El Paso** — A heavily fortified border city whose surviving population has developed an extensive network of tunnels and trade routes through the surrounding desert.
    

### Weird Fallout-style twists

- **Mutant longhorns** become enormous, aggressive herd animals.
    
- **Radscorpions** are replaced or supplemented by giant mutated tarantulas.
    
- **Deathclaws** inhabit abandoned ranchland and oil fields.
    
- **Vault 77:** **The Sunken Vault** — A Vault built beneath a dried-up reservoir begins flooding after decades underground. The residents must choose between opening the Vault to the wasteland or staying sealed while their home slowly collapses.
    
- **Vault 88:** Its residents were given competing versions of Texas history and told that only one version was true.
    
- **The Buc-ee’s cult:** A surprisingly well-organized post-war society formed around a surviving chain of enormous travel centers. Their beaver mascot has become a quasi-religious symbol.
    
- **The Oil Sea:** A gigantic contaminated lake formed from decades of leaking petroleum infrastructure.
    
- **The Last Rodeo:** A settlement holds an annual gladiatorial rodeo where people fight mutated animals and each other.

## Fallout: Lone Star

**Setting:** Texas, around 2290–2295  
**Tone:** Western frontier + post-apocalyptic road movie + weird Texas mythology

### 1. The map

Instead of trying to cover the entire state equally, divide it into several distinct regions:

- **The Houston Wasteland** — flooded suburbs, ruined refineries, chemical swamps, and enormous mutated bayou creatures.
    
- **Dallas–Fort Worth** — dense urban ruins dominated by competing factions fighting over pre-war corporate technology.
    
- **San Antonio** — a heavily fortified settlement built around the ruins of the Alamo.
    
- **Austin** — a bizarre artistic/technological enclave where musicians, hackers, and scientists have created an eccentric republic.
    
- **West Texas** — enormous empty spaces, abandoned oil fields, ghost towns, and roaming caravans.
    
- **The Rio Grande** — a dangerous frontier with settlements on both sides and a heavily contested river.
    
- **The Panhandle** — flat grasslands dominated by enormous mutated cattle and dust storms.
    
- **The Gulf Coast** — half-submerged cities and mutated marine life.
    

## 2. A major faction: The Texas Rangers

Not the baseball team—the **Texas Rangers**, revived as a post-war law-enforcement order.

They've spent generations protecting settlements and maintaining trade routes. They have a romanticized idea of the old Texas Rangers and wear distinctive leather/metal armor.

But there's a catch:

**They disagree about what Texas should become.**

One faction wants a decentralized frontier where towns govern themselves. Another believes Texas needs a powerful centralized government.

You could make the player's choices determine which philosophy becomes dominant.

## 3. The Republic of Texas — again

A group of settlers has created a new **Republic of Texas**, complete with its own constitution, elections, currency, and military.

They aren't simply "the good guys."

Their big problem is that they've inherited enormous amounts of pre-war infrastructure but don't have enough people to maintain it.

Their leadership wants:

> **Oil. Water. Electricity.**

And they're willing to make some uncomfortable alliances to obtain them.

## 4. The Houston faction: The Petrochemists

Deep beneath Houston, a surviving industrial complex has become a city-state.

Its inhabitants have preserved pre-war petroleum technology and are obsessed with keeping the oil industry alive.

They manufacture:

- fuel
    
- plastics
    
- explosives
    
- chemicals
    
- synthetic materials
    

Their society is incredibly wealthy compared with surrounding settlements—but horribly polluted.

Their ultimate project:

**Restarting a massive offshore oil platform.**

Nobody knows what will happen if they succeed.

## 5. The weird faction: The Church of the Eternal Star

This would be your classic _Fallout_ bizarre religion.

They worship the stars and believe the nuclear war was humanity's punishment for abandoning its destiny among them.

Their headquarters is an abandoned NASA facility near Houston.

They're convinced that the old space program left behind a secret spacecraft capable of reaching orbit.

And they're not entirely wrong.

## 6. A space-program storyline 🚀

You could have a major questline involving **NASA's surviving facilities**.

The player discovers that a pre-war experimental spacecraft was hidden underground.

Several factions want it:

- The Texas Republic wants it as a symbol of technological independence.
    
- The Church wants to launch it.
    
- Scientists want to study it.
    
- Raiders want its technology.
    
- A mysterious faction wants to use it for something nobody understands.
    

Eventually, the player gets to decide what happens to the program.

## 7. Texas-specific creatures

Some fun mutated wildlife:

### Deathhorns

Mutated longhorn cattle.

Basically enormous, extremely aggressive cattle with radiation-hardened horns.

### Gatorclaws

Mutated alligators, especially common around Houston and the Gulf.

### Armadillos

Tiny ones are harmless.

Huge mutated ones?

**Absolutely not.**

### Brahmin

Texas already gives you a perfect excuse for brahmin.

You could have particularly large **Longhorn Brahmin** used as pack animals.

### Dust Devils

Not creatures—living radioactive storms.

They appear randomly in West Texas and can completely change visibility during exploration.

## 8. The Alamo

The Alamo could be one of the game's most recognizable locations.

After the war, someone rebuilt it into a massive fortress.

Inside is an entire settlement.

The weird part?

Nobody actually agrees on what happened during the rebuilding.

There are several competing historical traditions, each claiming their ancestors saved San Antonio.

You could uncover what really happened.

## 9. Austin

Austin could be _extremely_ weird.

Imagine a settlement built around:

- old music venues
    
- university laboratories
    
- tech companies
    
- food trucks
    
- underground bunkers
    

Its residents have developed a culture centered around music and technology.

One faction controls the old university.

Another controls the power grid.

Another controls the music scene.

And there's a legendary pre-war AI somewhere beneath the city that everyone thinks is just an urban legend.

## 10. The borderlands

Rather than making the Mexico–Texas border simply a factional conflict, make it an enormous trade corridor.

Caravans travel along the old highways carrying:

- purified water
    
- ammunition
    
- medicine
    
- pre-war electronics
    
- food
    

Settlements on both sides depend on each other.

But something has begun attacking caravans.

The mystery eventually leads to an enormous pre-war automated defense network that still believes the war is ongoing.

## 11. A really _Fallout_ ending

The game's central question could be:

> **What should Texas become?**

By the final act, several competing visions emerge:

**The Republic:** centralized government.

**The Rangers:** decentralized frontier society.

**The Petrochemists:** industrial expansion.

**The Austin coalition:** technological development.

**The independent settlements:** no grand government at all.

Rather than having a simple good/evil choice, the ending could show how your decisions affected different regions.
